The firm

Acquire. Operate. Hold long-term.

We acquire profitable, established companies that meet our investment criteria. We then operate them, tightening operations, building the systems they need to scale and growing the business, and we hold for the long term.

Investment criteria

What we acquire.

We are looking for profitable, established companies with substantial operating tenure and well-diversified revenue, which we can optimise and scale, and that are tech-resilient.

Profile

  • Revenue of $3M to $20M a year
  • EBITDA of $1M or more
  • Diversified revenue, with the top 20% of customers accounting for less than 80% of the total
  • Revenue that holds independently of the owner’s own relationships
  • At least three years of steady results, ten or more preferred

Three pillars

Model optimisation

Companies where value can be created by optimising the business model. That opportunity typically sits in information spread across multiple systems, processes that remain manual, and pricing or capacity decisions taken on limited data.

Scalable

Businesses whose unit economics improve with scale and whose model can be extended once it has been optimised. Additional units, adjacent lines of revenue, new geographies, moving up the value chain and consolidating a fragmented sector are among the routes we consider.

Tech-disruption resilience

Businesses whose value creation is structurally difficult to displace, because it is delivered through physical execution and local presence rather than through information alone. Companies that trade principally on specialised knowledge, and companies that produce digital content, sit outside the mandate.

Approach

After an acquisition.

We acquire companies that are already well run, and keep running them. Results are measured over a long horizon rather than against a defined holding period.

Offers are built from sustainable earnings and the consistency of those earnings.

How the companies are run

Accountability sits lowProfit and loss responsibility goes as far down as it will go. A location, a branch or a route runs as its own unit, with its own numbers and its own decisions on staffing and stock.
Books close dailyUnits report daily rather than monthly. Decisions are taken on what is happening now, not on what happened five weeks ago.
Pay follows resultsA meaningful share of management pay is variable and tied to the performance of the unit the manager actually controls.
The centre stays smallHead office is kept deliberately thin. Support functions are funded by the units that use them.
Measure before changingThe first work after an acquisition is understanding where the money is made.

Portfolio

Two companies.

Names and locations are not disclosed.

Salon chain

Founded more than twenty years ago by a hair stylist, with one location. The founder’s son and daughter later joined and grew it to around eighteen locations, producing close to $10M in annual revenue. The family was approaching retirement. We acquired 100% in an all-cash transaction in November 2025.

In the first six months of our ownership the chain grew from eighteen locations to twenty-four. We opened a specialty format of high-end salons offering a deliberately narrow set of services, each run by a celebrity stylist who publishes their work on social media. In their first month they became the highest-grossing locations in the group.

We rebuilt the technology. The company ran on nine separate systems from nine vendors. We consolidated the data into a single client-centred view and have begun replacing the systems themselves with software built in-house using AI. We now employ our own development team.

Wholesale distribution

A consolidation. We acquired an anchor business and have been adding independent operations to it. Two so far. In each case we acquired the exclusive supply contracts, the customer base and the sales team.

Both were bought in decline. The first had been owned by someone running four other operations, with little oversight of this one; monthly sales had fallen to around $100,000 from roughly $250,000. The second had lost its head of sales and, with them, distribution of one of its principal brands; monthly sales had fallen to around $100,000 as well, against closer to $300,000 several years earlier.

We hired that former head of sales and are rebuilding the sales organisation, which now numbers close to twenty representatives. Two further operations are under consideration.

Tech-disruption resilience

Resilience to AI

Law, financial advice, design and architecture sit at the left of the horizontal axis, and video production, editing and digital marketing with them. Transport, emergency response, field and home services and personal care sit at the right.

Resilience to e-commerce

Standard goods that ship without fitting, trial or customisation sit at the foot of the vertical axis. Work that has to be performed, fitted or experienced in person sits at the top, along with goods that arrive with the service that installs or maintains them.
